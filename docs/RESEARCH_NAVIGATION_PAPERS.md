# Research: RL navigation generalization — training distribution, representation, action interface

**Status:** research deliverable only (mimo-navigation-papers mission), 2026-10-02. Owned files: this document and `results/mimo-navigation-papers/**`. No code, training, or asset changes were made.

**Scope boundary.** Distinct from [RESEARCH_TRAINING.md](RESEARCH_TRAINING.md) (PPO clip/gradient diagnostics) and [RESEARCH_NEXT_PHASE.md](RESEARCH_NEXT_PHASE.md) (PLR/PAIRED/ACCEL/GR2PO, occupancy-map branch). This file answers one question: **what must change in the training distribution, representation, or action interface for held-out navigation to improve.** Nine primary papers were found via 3 Google AI Mode queries (raw answers preserved verbatim: `results/mimo-navigation-papers/ai-1.md`, `ai-2.md`, `ai-3.md`) and then re-verified against arXiv/IEEE/GitHub sources — this round re-read the **Methods sections** of all nine to verify the training algorithm. Google answers were leads only; every row below comes from the primary source. Author/mechanism/number corrections to the Google claims are recorded in `results/mimo-navigation-papers/verification.md` (e.g. the "91.88%" figure is not from NavRL; "LoGoPlanner, Yang et al., ICRA 2026" is Peng et al., arXiv Dec 2025).

## Lead finding

The reviewed papers suggest three reusable directions. Their optimizers, representations and control interfaces differ; reported gains belong to those systems and do not establish a result for this project:

1. **Training-only privileged spatial supervision.** Time-of-arrival gradient direction + heading loss (Lee et al. 2025: 86% success, +34% over their baselines, concave-region timeouts removed, 589 m collision-free hardware flights), privileged anchor/ego-state auxiliaries (GUIDE), privileged CPA risk maps (ARR). The signal is a *training target*, stripped at deployment.
2. **Control of the task distribution.** Feasibility-guaranteed generators plus learning-progress-gated task switching (Xing et al. 2026 report 7.4× their zero-shot score metric; *random* switching −2.96% and adaptive +10.29% — both measured with L2 regularization disabled, their §4), success-gated density ramps (NavRL: dynamic obstacles 60→120 once success >80%), staged curricula whose removal is their worst ablation (Sun et al. 2026: one-step training "converge[s] to a local optimum and fail[s] to accomplish any tasks").
3. **Timing/perception robustness training.** NavRL++'s perturbation-aware fine-tuning (input latency ≤0.1 s, action latency ≤0.04 s, velocity-gain ±40%, Bernoulli obstacle dropout p=0.3, lr 5e-5) produced gains **only when paired with short-horizon temporal input**: PAFT on their single-step conv policy *lowered* success (NavRL-IT-PF 66.42 vs NavRL-IT 99.59 at low complexity), while PAFT + transformer (NavRL++) improved it; the transformer backbone alone cut control effort 0.093 → 0.043 m/s² (NavRL-IT → NavRL-IT-T).

**Training-algorithm census (methods checked by the delegated researcher):** 7 of 9 use PPO — NavRL (clip 0.1, MLP, Beta action head), NavRL++ (PPO+GAE, transformer), ARR (PPO, Isaac Sim/OmniDrones), GUIDE (PPO+GAE, asymmetric actor-critic), Xing et al. (multi-task parallel PPO), Sun et al. (PPO, CNN+GRU), TRACED (PPO student). Two do **not**: Lee et al.'s privileged-ToA policy is trained by **Analytical Policy Gradient through differentiable point-mass dynamics** (BPTT, no critic, GRU cell fed by 192-d feature vectors, thrust-vector + predicted-yaw action — arXiv 2509.08177 §III-A/D), and LoGoPlanner is **not RL at all** (two-stage supervised training on ~200k A*-refined trajectories with a diffusion trajectory head). None of the three levers above depends on which optimizer is used, so PPO stays our control.

The action-interface conclusion is negative and useful: velocity-based commands behind a fast low-level controller is what the non-racing papers use — NavRL outputs a 3D velocity vector (no yaw-rate term in the paper), ARR outputs continuous velocity, GUIDE outputs twist `[vx,vy,ωz]`; racing papers use CTBR only in the faster regime, and Lee et al. use thrust+heading. Nothing found argues for changing `[vx,vy,vz,yaw_rate]` → frozen RAPTOR (20 Hz policy / 100 Hz controller), though representation/memory changes are admissible on evidence (see "PPO control" below).

**Hypothesis (each has a falsifier below):** the three failures separate into (H1) missing progress-around-obstacle *credit* → choice 1 (arm (b) conditional on the reachability audit); (H2) wrong *shape* of the task distribution and difficulty ramp → choice 2; (H3) command *timing/response* mismatch between sim and Webots → choice 3.

**Unknown:** the physics-mismatch vs policy-timing split behind the 8 divergences; whether corners are also representation-limited (action/observation reachability — the native Luna agent's open question), which no paper here settles.

**Overall falsifier:** if matched arms of choices 1–3 cannot raise held-out rooms to ≥25/30 with corners >0/30 at declared budgets while keeping Webots contacts ≤12/30, then the lever is not distribution/representation/timing, and effort should move to the reachability question or the map/frontier branch of `RESEARCH_NEXT_PHASE.md`.

## Ranked implementation choices

Ranking rationale: (1) attacks the largest *measured* hole with pre-built infrastructure and no deployment truth — **but it is conditional**, see below; (2) is required for the user's longer/narrower courses and is the only verified anti-forgetting mechanism found; (3) is the only verified remedy for the transfer/timing symptom class and is the precondition for later speed work (smoother commands → headroom to raise the cap without losing doors). All three keep **PPO as the control** and change one thing each.

**Ordering condition (not assumed first):** choice 1's arm (b) supervises a *globally directed* target (`+∇Φ`). That target can be **observation-aliased** (identical local depth, opposite correct detour — e.g. goal behind a wall on either side) or **unreachable** in the deployed observation, in which case a training-only loss asks the policy to infer something its inputs cannot carry. The native Luna agent's reachability/expressibility audit decides: if that audit reports the required detour directions are inferable from depth+ego+goal, run order 1 → 2 as ranked; if it reports aliasing or unreachable cases, run arm (a) only (potential-difference reward shaping, no directional target loss), or start with choice 2 and re-rank afterwards. Record which branch ran.

### 1. Training-only geodesic direction supervision (ToA-style) on the existing potential grid

- **Addresses rooms / corners / transfer:** **Rooms** — primary (routing half: final goal alone scores 0/30, witness waypoints 30/30); **Corners** — partial (detour credit; waypoints only reach 9/30, so a local-control gap remains); **Transfer** — no claim; re-check the selected checkpoint on Webots as a guardrail.
- **Sign convention (corrected):** the existing grid stores `Φ = −distance/cap` with `Φ = 0` at the goal (`training_potential.hpp:289`, saturation `−0.99999994`, invalid cells `≤ −1`). The toward-goal direction is therefore **`normalize(+∇Φ)`**, *not* `−∇Φ`. Mapping to Lee et al.: their target-velocity direction is `v_set = normalize(−∇T)` where `T` is time-of-arrival (grows with travel distance), and since `Φ = −d/cap`, `+∇Φ ≡ −∇d ≡ −∇T` up to the positive scale `1/cap`. So the arm-(b) target is **`normalize(+∇Φ)`**, which *is* their `−∇T` — same direction, opposite sign to their `∇T`.
- **Minimum change:** trainer-side only. Two nested arms: **(a)** potential-difference reward shaping from the existing `training_potential.hpp` grid (already staged in `RESEARCH_NEXT_PHASE.md`, static families 14–16 only); **(b)** the Lee et al. analogue — a loss supervising the policy's velocity/yaw toward `normalize(+∇Φ)` (target-velocity/direction term), optionally with heading alignment, **run only if the reachability audit above clears it**. Actor inputs, the deployed observation, RAPTOR, and the action seam are unchanged: the geodesic gradient is a training target, never an input (the guided actor's *goal*-direction slots stay, since goal direction is computable at deployment from pose + goal).
- **Export note:** the *"184-input, 64-hidden, four-action"* contract (`main.mm:810`) is the **current implementation**, not an immutable requirement. Arm (a)/(b) need no export change; if a later arm justifiably needs representation or memory change (recurrent state, occupancy/geodesic channel, richer goal encoding), that is an admissible design proposal that must be declared as an implementation change with its own paired evidence — not ruled out by the contract.
- **Hypothesis:** room/corner failure is dominated by absent progress-around-obstacle credit — goal direction gives zero or anti-signal behind walls, and locally the privileged-waypoint jump (0/30 → 30/30 rooms) says routing, not control, is the room gap; Lee et al. show the same signature (a policy that can yaw but has no ToA term still times out in concave regions).
- **Control:** current recipe (λ0 / no direction term), matched seed bank, matched transitions, identical DEV evaluation; PPO objective, families, and scoring untouched; oracle diagnostics excluded from selection.
- **Falsifier:** at matched transitions, rich-dev rooms ≤25/30 with corners still 0/30 (f14 = 0), **or** Webots contacts rise above 12/30, **or** the term reproduces the training collapse or increases door-panel hits (Lee et al.'s FOV warning: a gradient can point where the camera is not — inspect the door-edge contact class explicitly), **or** the reachability audit (above) reports the target as unlearnable from the deployed observation — that last outcome is itself a falsifier of arm (b), not a tuning problem.
- **Real metric:** per-family rich-dev success (rooms/corners/vertical) at the declared 20 s / 1.5 m/s budget; f14; independent Webots 30-room success + contacts (baseline 18/30, 12 contacts, 8 divergences); collision 0.18 m / goal 0.35 m unchanged; no new task budgets (60 s waypoint budget stays diagnostic-only).
- **Cost:** training-only, potential cache already built with CPU/Metal parity; one matched arm in the family-15 budget class (~1.2M transitions) serialized under `results/metal-training.lock`; no actor, export, or deployment change.

### 2. Distribution shape: feasibility-guaranteed concave/narrow/long-route bank with learning-progress gating

- **Addresses rooms / corners / transfer:** **Corners** — primary (f14 = 0 is a coverage + discovery failure; spawn diversity and staged difficulty are what Sun et al./Xing et al. credit); **Rooms** — guardrail only (must not drop the 25/30 floor); **Transfer** — no direct claim, re-check selected checkpoint; also targets the user's longer varied courses and narrow passages, and the documented forgetting (door vs table rehearsal tradeoff, later collapse).
- **Minimum change:** world/generator + sampler only: **(i)** make "a traversable path exists" an explicit generator invariant (Sun et al. guarantee it; our generator has rejection statistics but no published invariant); **(ii)** spawn heading and start-point diversity so openings are sometimes outside the forward camera view (Sun et al. random-init; our documented family-14 case: opening outside FOV, initial lateral detour required); **(iii)** gate the difficulty/family mix on measured learning progress (Xing et al.'s Spearman/Kalman reward-flatness; NavRL's success-threshold density ramp) instead of fixed proportions. This is **not** PLR seed reweighting (the `RESEARCH_NEXT_PHASE.md` first test): it changes *which topologies and difficulty levels exist and when they ramp*, not which seed of a fixed bank is replayed; keep that document's per-family quotas as the retention guardrail and declare the run order.
- **Control:** current fixed-quota sampler (documented 500-rollout control: uniform → 5/30 rooms, 27/30 vertical, both 0/30 corners), matched seeds and transitions; final split untouched; new holdout topologies never used for selection.
- **Falsifier:** corners remain 0/30 across ≥3 matched seeds at the declared budget; or rooms/vertical fall beyond the predeclared tolerance (5 points suggested in `RESEARCH_NEXT_PHASE.md`); or gating itself degrades the peak — Xing et al. show *random* switching hurt (measured with L2 regularization disabled, their §4), so the gated arm must beat both no-switch and random-switch arms under our fixed regularization.
- **Real metric:** f14 corner success at 1.5 m/s / 20 s; room floor ≥25/30 at selection; vertical retention; per-family (never pooled) success; selected-checkpoint Webots re-check; any new diagnostic budget (e.g. 60 s) declared separately, never as a baseline relaxation.
- **Cost:** generator + sampler changes in the world/family-sampling area plus one or two matched arms; moderate implementation; no actor/deploy change; interacts with the PLR test, so sequence must be declared up front.

### 3. Perturbation-aware training for command timing/response (NavRL++-style), paired with the existing temporal cache

- **Addresses rooms / corners / transfer:** **Transfer** — primary (12/30 hard contacts, 8 Metal-success→Webots-contact divergences); door-edge contact class (contact 20 ms after an unsafe command, i.e. inside one 20 Hz command interval) and the attitude/floor excursion (±velocity-gain randomization is the paper-side twin of our halved-speed finding); partially moving obstacles (perception-dropout robustness). **Rooms/corners** — no routing claim; must hold the 25/30 floor.
- **Minimum change:** env/trainer randomization only, actor untouched: input latency 0–100 ms (0–2 nav steps at 20 Hz), action latency 0–40 ms, ±40% velocity-gain / command-response mismatch, and the dropout analogue — **NavRL++'s own perturbations are state-side** (Gaussian input noise; Bernoulli *dynamic-obstacle detection* dropout p=0.3), where perception failure was their **largest single effect (>5% SR drop alone)**, input noise/latency/control disturbance each cost ~1–3%, and the combination cost >10% (>15% high-density); the depth-pixel-hole variant is *our* mapping of that idea onto a depth-only observation, so it is a new arm, not a paper result. Optionally run as a short reduced-lr fine-tune from the selected checkpoint (NavRL++'s post-training pattern) instead of a full retrain — note their fine-tune is ~10–15 GPU-h per curriculum stage on top of a ~200 GPU-h total, so a matched full retrain is the honest comparison for us. NavRL++'s pairing condition matters: perturbations alone did **not** help their single-step conv policy and in fact cut its success (NavRL-IT-PF 66.42 vs NavRL-IT 99.59). Our guided actor already carries current + previous pooled depth with a ~0.4 s geometry cache, and `main.mm:517` provides mode 18 (depth-only vs cache) — but it is explicitly *"an inference ablation, not a matched retrain"*, so budget a matched retrain arm before claiming the cache's contribution.
- **Control:** current clean-trained selected-checkpoint recipe (rooms-focused 25/30 lineage), matched seeds/transitions, identical clean-Metal evaluation; PPO objective and scoring unchanged.
- **Falsifier:** Webots contacts stay ≥12/30 at equal-or-better Metal success; or Metal rooms fall below the 25/30 floor; or randomization reproduces the best-peak collapse; or replaying the diagnostic cases shows no drop in "contact within one command interval of the first unsafe command".
- **Real metric:** Webots transfer success/contacts from Luna's 30-room matrix (baseline 18/30 / 12 contacts / 8 divergences); count of post-unsafe-command contacts; command smoothness (per-step Δv as the control-effort proxy); Metal DEV rooms floor; speed cap stays 1.5 (1.75 remains non-default: faster but lost 3 hard room cases).
- **Cost:** randomization flags plus an optional reduced-lr fine-tune — cheaper than a full run; one or two matched arms under the lock; no observation, export, or deployment change; no deployment truth.

## PPO control vs training-only privileged / oracle variants

- **Control:** PPO with the current recipe. Each choice above changes exactly one thing (reward term; task distribution; env randomization) and is compared against a matched PPO arm. The Methods re-read found 7/9 PPO, 1 APG (Lee et al., needs a differentiable point-mass simulator we do not have), 1 supervised-diffusion (LoGoPlanner) — **no paper here offers a reason to leave PPO**, and none of the three levers depends on the optimizer.
- **Training-only privileged signals permitted** (all detached/stripped at export): geodesic potential / direction loss (choice 1), privileged anchor- or risk-map auxiliaries in the GUIDE/ARR pattern, privileged critic inputs. Export constraint for now: *"navigation export requires a 184-input, 64-hidden, four-action actor"* (`main.mm:810`) — this describes **today's implementation**, and any arm that changes the actor's representation or memory (recurrent state, occupancy/geodesic channel, richer goal encoding) is a legitimate evidence-driven proposal that must be declared as an implementation change with paired arms, not rejected by citing the contract. The hard invariant is narrower: no world truth, no witness route, no geodesic/ToA field at inference.
- **Oracle / diagnostic, never success and never deployment:** `challenge_evaluation.hpp::run_witness` witness routes; privileged intermediate waypoints (30/30 rooms etc.); the 60 s waypoint budget. The deployed actor keeps depth + ego + goal only.
- **Action interface: keep `[vx,vy,vz,yaw_rate]` → frozen RAPTOR** (20 Hz / 100 Hz) as the default seam. Support in the papers is structural rather than exact: NavRL outputs a 3D velocity vector with no yaw-rate term, ARR outputs continuous velocity, GUIDE outputs twist `[vx,vy,ωz]`; CTBR appears only in the racing papers; Lee et al. use thrust+heading over body-rate control. No paper found argues for changing our seam — and equally, none is a byte-for-byte match, so a representation/observation change remains open on evidence (above).

## Considered and not chosen now

- **VO / action safety shield (deploy-legal, depth only) — deferred, not dismissed.** NavRL ships an LP velocity-obstacle shield as a first-class deploy component and reports it as a contribution, so the mechanism is evidence-backed rather than merely a metric trick. The right way to judge it is by measurement, not by a Goodhart objection: if/when run, use a **2×2 report with the unfiltered PPO control preserved** — {filter off, filter on} × {training recipe A, training recipe B} — and record **task completion (rooms/corners/vertical success), efficiency (time, path length, per-step Δv), and contacts** for each cell, so a contact drop bought by lost progress or lost efficiency is visible. Also report filtered-vs-unfiltered contacts on the same checkpoint (the filter's own effect) separately from any training change. Placement: optional after choice 3, since the door-edge class is where it would first be measured.
- **Occupancy/frontier map + backtracking:** already the prescribed branch of `RESEARCH_NEXT_PHASE.md` — not duplicated here. Choice 1 is the smaller supervised-signal step before it, consistent with `RESEARCH_FIDELITY.md` (reward-only geodesic before architectural memory). Note the reachability condition above: if the Luna audit finds global-direction targets unreachable from the deployed observation, this branch (architectural memory) becomes the ranked successor rather than a later upgrade.
- **Recurrent PPO / LSTM:** the Berkeley LSTM64 precedent is already assessed in `RESEARCH_FIDELITY.md:189` (dead-end failures remain); it changes rollout/hidden-state handling; the only new evidence (Liu & Wang, two-frame LiDAR + CNN-LSTM + shield, 91.88% sim-only) has no real-robot or depth-camera evidence and no primary URL resolved, so it is recorded in verification notes rather than adopted. Adjacent new evidence: GUIDE and Lee et al. both use recurrent state (8-frame buffers / GRU) and CRL uses a GRU — supportive of memory in general, but none isolates recurrence as the cause of a gain.
- **UED / TRACED machinery:** no aerial evidence; `RESEARCH_NEXT_PHASE.md` already defers UED until fixed-bank replay stalls.
- **LoGoPlanner-style metric geometry + diffusion planner:** replaces the velocity action interface, is supervised (not RL), and its method keeps an explicit metric-scale reconstruction as intermediate memory — so it is not a "map-free PPO" precedent. Its auxiliary-loss idea is already covered by choice 1; its representation idea (metric geometry memory) is the same direction as the occupancy branch.
- **GR2PO: not re-assessed.** No new primary evidence found — the 7 PPO papers all use actor-critic PPO variants, and the two non-PPO methods (APG, supervised diffusion) are not group-relative or critic-free — so the `RESEARCH_NEXT_PHASE.md` decision stands unchanged.

## Zero-shot acceptance boundary

The operator requires transfer without additional target-side training. Perturbation training or fine-tuning proposed here must occur in the source Metal training pipeline before weights and navigation logic are frozen. Webots development scores may inform source experiments, but final transfer tests require new blind target worlds. No target-simulator fine-tuning, test-seed tuning or oracle deployment input can count toward that proof. Current results remain 25/30 selected Metal development rooms versus 18/30 Webots development rooms; the full acceptance goal is not achieved.
