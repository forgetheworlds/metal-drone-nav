# Next-phase research: broader local navigation

**Decision:** Keep PPO as the training control. First test whether a small replay bank can improve the current mixed curriculum without losing skills across doors, tables, and moving threats. Do not replace PPO first. The independent Webots integration is a parallel transfer-validation effort, not a training algorithm replacement. The current gap is task coverage and retention, not a lack of policy-update methods.

## What the current evidence says

The trainer now has a strong baseline: 128 environments, 32 navigation steps per rollout, two PPO epochs, 256-sample minibatches, RAPTOR and 100 Hz physics in the loop. The retained raw-actor update takes about0.026s onM3 after the measured forward/reduction optimizations; the guided geometry-memory workload is typically about0.03–0.04s in recent run logs. The earlier0.295s result was an intermediate implementation. The current actor uses perfect simulated ego state with depth. This means additional learning iterations are cheap, but state-estimation error and long-route structure remain out of the training distribution. See [docs/BENCHMARKS.md](BENCHMARKS.md), [main.mm](../main.mm), and [sim.metal](../sim.metal).

The distribution has already caused forgetting. Door rehearsal improved held two-door success but reduced table success; stress rehearsal improved command-delay doors while losing clean-door and table performance. `world.hpp::wtraining_family` currently samples family 12 as 50% single-door, 25% table, 25% broad family 0–6; family 13 samples 33% door, 17% table, 17% broad, and 33% fast approach/crossing threats. In training families 12/13, `sim_clean_training_env` makes even-numbered environments clean and odd-numbered environments stressed. Latest root-run results (1 October 2026; not yet in the benchmark table) show training raised 2 m/s crossing at nominal 1 s TTC from 0% to 100%, and 2 m/s approach at 0.5 s TTC from 0% to 75%. A 0.5 s fast crossing still fails, and a new joint model lowered mixed static success from 94% to 84%. Keep these preliminary values separate from the earlier packaged-checkpoint results.

The current evaluation exposes the gaps that should define the next experiments: the original selected policy scored6.25% on an unseen single-door family and 44.53% on table/counter cases before later geometry-memory improvements; high-speed crossing at short TTC remained difficult. The target tasks should therefore vary doorway clearance, overhead and table clearance, route turns, view occlusion, crossing direction, actual path TTC, sensor timing, and ego-state quality. A longer connected route should replace some short 3–4 m point-to-point rooms. Keep a complete-route feasibility witness so generator changes do not create impossible tasks. ## Training frontier and failure replay

**Start with Prioritized Level Replay (PLR), implemented narrowly around the existing generators.** PLR stores seeded training levels, scores the latest trajectory on each level by its mean absolute GAE (equivalent to an L1 value error in the paper’s PPO setting), and preferentially replays levels with high scores. It also mixes in a staleness distribution so levels with old scores return to training. The authors found that rank-based prioritization worked better than greedy or raw proportional prioritization in their Procgen experiments. PLR requires only that the simulator can deterministically recreate a level from its identifier; it does not require an adversary or a new policy architecture. [PLR paper, ICML 2021](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/jiang21b.html) · [reference implementation pinned at `ccecf452ee3342217ece964aaf10c2831625f9b3`](https://github.com/facebookresearch/level-replay/tree/ccecf452ee3342217ece964aaf10c2831625f9b3)

For this trainer, a level key must identify the full task: generator family and seed, goal/start configuration, moving-object configuration, and sensor/controller stress settings. `WWorld.seed` alone is insufficient when delay, dropout, noise, or wind changes the MDP. Score only complete episodes, or accumulate the score until the episode ends; episodes can span multiple 32-step rollouts. Use raw GAE before advantage normalization. Keep the existing PPO objective, actor, geometry prior, and held-out evaluator unchanged.

The first test should hold the family distribution constant and change only seed selection within each family. Compare the current uniform seeded sampler with a 50/50 split between uniform levels and rank-prioritized replay with a staleness component. Preserve a minimum uniform share within every door, table, broad-scene, and threat stratum. This 50/50 setting is a test arm, not a claim that PLR’s paper selected it for drones. Do not let a single family occupy the whole replay pool. The recent door/table tradeoff makes family quotas essential.

Use a fixed training bank of parameterized seeds. Add door gap and offset, table/counter height and under-clearance, turns per route length, view occlusion, moving direction and relative speed, and path-based TTC as labeled fields. Reuse the current exact family generators for the initial bank. Add state-estimation perturbations as a separate factor: temporally correlated position/yaw bias and timestamp error. Keep physical truth for collision scoring only. Save the generator version and parameters with each bank entry so a failure reproduces exactly.

For each arm, use the same initialization, PPO batch/epoch settings, total transitions, and M3 runtime. Run at least three matched seeds. Report per-family success, collision, timeout, completion time including failures, progress, minimum clearance, and success versus speed/TTC. Keep two-door combinations and new multi-room route seeds held out. Select checkpoints by a per-family success floor or minimum-family success; do not choose by one pooled mean. A replay change passes only if it improves the failure strata and keeps the current static/mixed families within a predeclared tolerance, such as five percentage points. Compare both fixed-transition and fixed-wall-time results.

PLR is preferable to automatic adversarial level generation as the first test. PAIRED uses protagonist, antagonist, and environment-design policies to maximize regret while avoiding unsolvable levels; ACCEL adds local level mutations to a replay curriculum. Both need more machinery and valid-level tests than a seed bank. They become reasonable only if fixed replay shows that the frontier stops moving. [PAIRED, NeurIPS 2020](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2020/hash/985e9a46e10005356bbaf194249f6856-Abstract.html) · [ACCEL, ICML 2022](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/parker-holder22a.html) · [archived UED implementation](https://github.com/facebookresearch/dcd)

## GR2PO: real method, poor first change

The relevant continuous-control method is **Group Relative Return Policy Optimization (GR2PO)**, arXiv v1 2609.19850, submitted 17 September 2026. It is different from simply applying the LLM GRPO loss to drone actions. GR2PO collects N independent parallel rollouts and treats the N returns at each same time index as a group; group members do not need the same starting state. It calculates full discounted Monte Carlo returns backward, normalizes each time slice across environments, clips the resulting relative advantage, then uses PPO’s clipped probability-ratio objective with entropy and KL-based early stopping. It removes the learned critic. At a rollout boundary it sets the return tail to zero; it does not bootstrap a value estimate.

The paper tests Ant-v5, Walker2d-v5, and Humanoid-v5 for 80–400 million transitions with five seeds. GR2PO beats its immediate-reward GRPO baseline, but PPO has higher reported final evaluation return on all three tasks. The authors say they will release code after acceptance; no official code was linked in the paper I reviewed. The result is a promising method report, not evidence that it beats this project’s PPO. [GR2PO paper, arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19850v1)

There are two specific mismatches. First, the current rollout horizon is 32 steps at 20 Hz (1.6 s), while successful goal times are about 2.5–2.9 s. Zero-tail Monte Carlo returns would cut off many successful trajectories before their outcome. Second, same-time groups in the current family-13 mixture contain doors, tables, broad scenes, threats, and clean/stressed conditions. Their raw returns are not directly comparable. Grouping by family or stress would reduce this confound but shrink the group, while a full 128-environment group repeats the confound. These are testable risks, not proof the method cannot work.

Do not replace the PPO control. If PLR and a broader task bank leave a measured critic bottleneck, run a separate GR2PO branch against a PPO branch on the same frozen seed bank, with matched total transitions and wall time. Use an episode-complete horizon or another explicitly matched terminal-return design, and report the zero-tail treatment. Track per-family quality, not just mean reward. The current project already stores returns and advantages; GR2PO removes critic training but changes the advantage estimator, group statistics, and episode-horizon behavior at the same time. Treat it as a method comparison, not a “free” critic deletion.

**External evaluation decision:** Do not install or vendor a benchmark for the next learning iteration. First port the FlightBench TO/VO/AOL ideas into deterministic local scenario metadata and add route topologies that target the current blind-corner and crossing failures. Any result must say whether it uses the built-in controller or the complete persistent-reference + RAPTOR stack. Keep complete scene families and start/goal routes out of training; public random seeds alone are not a strong holdout.

## Smallest next decision

1. Preserve today’s selected checkpoint and family-12/13 PPO run as the control.
2. Add a deterministic training-level bank keyed by full world and stress parameters, then test within-family PLR-style replay with a uniform floor. Keep family proportions, PPO, rollout size, and total interaction budget fixed.
3. Add long multi-room routes, measured TO/VO/AOL-like metadata, and correlated ego-state error as separate factors. Keep new route topologies and fast 0.5 s crossing scenes held out.
4. Accept the scheduler only if doorway/table/fast-crossing gains do not reduce mixed/static success beyond the preregistered family tolerance and improve time-to-threshold. Revisit GR2PO only after this data exists.

## Final-goal navigation through unknown routes

Autonomous final-goal navigation through unknown routes is part of this phase. Waypoint-following checks are diagnostics for local control, not a replacement definition of success. Family 14 exposes a real exploration failure: its route requires an initial lateral detour away from the final goal, while its opening is outside the forward camera view. The 8 m corner control completed 1,000 PPO rollouts (4.096 million transitions; about 82,729 episodes) with zero successful episodes and about 99.994% collisions. Its value loss was about 0.011, which shows that the critic fit failure-heavy returns; it does not show route discovery. A geometric witness is 14–15.7 m long and fits the 20 s cap at 1.5 m/s by distance alone, but geometry is not dynamic feasibility. The 184D guided actor sees current and previous pooled depth; its short geometry cache spans about 0.4 s. Neither supplies episode-long evidence of which opening was explored or whether a detour must be reversed. These results make more rollouts of the same setup a poor next experiment.

The privileged witness diagnostic in `challenge_evaluation.hpp::run_witness` supplies sequential witness goals while keeping the flight continuous, the frozen RAPTOR controller and physics live, and collision scoring enabled. At a 1 m/s speed cap with a 60 s budget, all 90 mirrored development routes passed with no collision or timeout. Mean route times were 21.92 s for corners, 19.75 s for rooms, and 21.87 s for vertical routes; worst-case time was 24.40 s. Minimum measured clearances were 0.083 m, 0.173 m, and 0.098 m for those groups. This establishes that those specific witness routes are dynamically flyable under privileged waypoint guidance at that cap and budget. It does not establish autonomous route discovery or feasibility under the original 1.5 m/s, 20 s budget, which is the current matched test. Next compare the frozen actor following the same local waypoint sequence; neither diagnostic counts as autonomous final-goal success.

For the autonomous test, the smallest justified persistent memory is a compact, sensor-built 3D occupancy/visited grid plus frontier selection and a known-free route to the next local subgoal. Keep the goal bearing as a selection preference, but allow a frontier that initially moves away from the goal; remember visited frontiers and failed approaches so selection can backtrack instead of repeating the same blocked route. Keep cells in three states—free, occupied, unknown. A depth ray can mark traversed space free and its valid endpoint occupied; missing, dropped, stale, or invalid depth must remain unknown. Use only depth, timestamp/confidence, and the vehicle's pose estimate to update the map. Do not read `WWorld.obstacles`, a witness, or hidden generator geometry in policy evaluation. `OctoMap` describes why free, occupied, and unknown distinctions matter in 3D maps; Yamauchi's frontier method selects the boundary of known free and unexplored space to extend an unknown-world map. These ideas fit the failure better than treating “not currently seen” as safe or adding a longer stack of frames. [OctoMap paper](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10514-012-9321-0) · [Yamauchi 1997 paper](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~motionplanning/papers/sbp_papers/integrated2/yamauchi_frontier_explor.pdf)

Use a bounded route-scale grid, not a general mapping framework in the Metal hot path. The current simulated operating volume is roughly 16 × 10 × 5 m. As a sizing example, 0.4 m voxels yield 40 × 25 × 13 = 13,000 cells, or about 13 KiB per environment for one byte per cell (about 1.6 MiB for 128 environments), before timestamps or extra channels. This is a memory estimate, not a measured update cost. Keep the current short-horizon depth/geometry prior for immediate collision avoidance. Put moving-object risk in a separate transient layer with measured-motion updates and expiry; do not let a stale moving hit permanently block the static route map, and do not treat expired space as free without a new valid ray. Initially use the existing actor's goal-direction/distance slots for the planner's next known-free local subgoal. This preserves the actor/action/RAPTOR seam and makes map/frontier errors inspectable. Build a per-episode map in a start-anchored frame from depth plus an explicit pose estimate; the current helper projects depth with noise-free simulator `RLPhysicsState` ego pose. This is an idealized ego-state input; it does not inspect obstacle metadata. Real estimator drift and timing error remain untested. Add pose noise/drift as a separate test and report that the current actor has perfect simulated ego state.

Bug-style wall-following is a useful low-code comparison, not the main remedy. Its classical completeness results assume a planar point robot, static unknown obstacles, and reliable boundary following with goal bearing. Those assumptions omit a quadrotor's vertical choices, dynamics, depth occlusion, and moving threats; family 14's required multi-turn route is exactly where a local boundary rule can fail to retain useful route history. [Lumelsky and Stepanov, 1986](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAC.1986.1104175) Recurrent policy state is a plausible smaller learned-memory ablation: DRQN showed recurrence can integrate observation history in partially observed tasks, but this is not evidence it will discover these 3D routes. It also changes PPO rollout/training to preserve hidden-state sequences and reset boundaries. The current feedforward navigation actor has no episode-persistent hidden state; `SimRun.hidden[16]` belongs to RAPTOR. [Hausknecht and Stone, 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.06527)

Run one fixed-seed, held-out topology comparison after the witness diagnostic: (A) current actor with its short geometry memory; (B) same actor and same local subgoal interface, but with persistent sensor-derived occupancy/frontier/backtracking; (C) Bug-style boundary-following as a diagnostic baseline. Keep training exposure, speed cap, mission time, and final-goal success rule identical. Report success, collision, timeout, completion time including failures, path length, explored-map coverage, repeated-frontier count, and mapping/planning wall time. A map branch is justified only if it raises held-out final-goal success without an unacceptable collision or time cost, and benefits come from the added sensor memory rather than access to witness or world geometry. Recurrent PPO is a later matched branch if map-based routing works but the actor still fails to use local subgoals or handle partial observations. No map, frontier planner, recurrent actor, or autonomous 8 m corner success is implemented or established by the current evidence.


## Waypoint contract and replay controls

The stricter 0.10m witness controller establishes route execution, but it is not a fair comparison with a policy trained to terminate within0.35m. With the ordinary0.35m waypoint acceptance, the same goal-script controller completes30/30 rooms and30/30 vertical routes, but collides onall30 corners because early waypoint advancement cuts the narrow turn. The learned policy with the same0.35m rule completes3/30 corners,1/30 rooms and10/30 vertical routes. These diagnostics use privileged witness waypoints and do not establish autonomous route discovery. They show that waypoint acceptance, stopping behavior and goal-direction coverage require attention as well as durable route memory.

The first500-rollout mirrored-bank controls use fixed per-environment family quotas and the same PPO settings/initial weights. Training now samples raw Gaussian latents and applies the deployed overhead gate before execution (mode22); log probabilities stay on raw latents, so the observation-dependent execution map does not alter the PPO likelihood calculation. Uniform sampling reaches5/30 development rooms and27/30 vertical routes at its selected checkpoint. Both controls retain0/30 corners. Priority selection has not established a benefit; this is one sampler seed, not an algorithm-level conclusion. The final split remains unused.

A bounded geodesic potential computed from training-world geometry is the next controlled route-discovery hypothesis. It supplies a reward term only; the actor and deployment must receive no distance field, witness route or hidden world geometry. Use the same discount for the potential difference, set terminal potential tozero, and retain the unshaped baseline. The potential-shaping theorem supports policy invariance under its assumptions, not a guarantee of faster exploration or transfer. Compare actual held-out outcomes and retention before adopting it.
